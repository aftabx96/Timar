PostGIS is an add on that teaches that database to understand maps.Without PostGIS, a location is just two numbers sitting in a table, and the database has no idea what they mean. With PostGIS, the database understands shapes and distance, so you can ask it real questions:which plots are inside this creek areahow many hectares does this plot coverwas this volunteer's GPS point actually inside the plot boundary, or did they submit from homewhich plots are within two kilometres of a reported cutting complaintThat last one is why you need it. Your whole project is about locations, so the database has to think in locations.Sentinel-2 imagery from CopernicusCopernicus is the European Union's earth observation programme. Sentinel-2 is a pair of satellites it operates that photograph the whole planet, and the images are free for anyone to download.Each pixel covers about ten metres of ground, and the same place is photographed again every few days. So you can pull up Korangi Creek from last month and from this month and compare them.The important part is that these satellites see more than a normal camera. They capture infrared light too, and healthy plants reflect infrared strongly while dead or cut vegetation does not. That is how you detect green cover loss without going there. Free data, no drone, no permission needed.RasterioA satellite image is not a normal JPG. It is a raster file, meaning a grid of numbers, with several layers for the different light bands, plus information about which real world coordinates each pixel sits on.Rasterio is the Python library that opens those files and lets you work with them. You use it to read the bands, cut out just the area of Karachi coast you care about, do the maths that turns raw bands into a vegetation health value, and compare one month against another.How the three work togetherRasterio opens the free Copernicus satellite images and calculates where green cover dropped. Those flagged areas get saved into PostgreSQL with PostGIS, which can then tell you which of your plots they fall inside. The platform shows that on the map as a possible cutting alert.One line for the defence: "PostGIS lets the database understand maps instead of just numbers, Sentinel-2 gives us free satellite imagery of the coast every few days, and rasterio is the Python library we use to read those images and measure change between months."
